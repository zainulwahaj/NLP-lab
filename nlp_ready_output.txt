introduction to natural language processing natural language processing usually shortened to nlp is the field of computer science that teaches machines to read understand and produce human language
every day people write emails send messages post reviews and search the web and nearly all of this data is unstructured text
nlp gives us the tools to turn that text into something a program can count compare and learn from
the field sits between linguistics computer science and machine learning
linguistics explains how words sentences and meaning are built
computer science provides the data structures and algorithms to process millions of documents
machine learning supplies the statistical methods that let a system improve from examples instead of following only hand written rules
a typical nlp project starts with raw text cleans it splits it into tokens and then represents the tokens as numbers
after that a model can classify the text translate it summarise it or answer questions about it
each of these stages can go wrong in its own way which is why a careful student of language technology learns to inspect the data at every step
a short history of nlp the first serious attempts at machine translation began in the s
in a public demonstration translated more than sixty russian sentences into english and many researchers believed the problem would be solved within a few years
language turned out to be far more ambiguous and irregular than early systems assumed
during the s and s most systems relied on hand written grammar rules
programs such as eliza imitated a conversation by matching keywords and they showed how easily people read intelligence into simple text patterns
rule based systems were precise on narrow topics but broke down as soon as the vocabulary grew
large collections of text became available and researchers began to count words and word sequences instead of writing every rule by hand
hidden markov models and n gram language models became standard tools
in the s neural networks took over first with word embeddings and then with transformer models trained on billions of words
text preprocessing raw text is messy
it contains capital letters punctuation web addresses such as and numbers like or and stray symbols
before any model sees the text it is usually cleaned
common steps are lowercasing removing noise with regular expressions splitting the text into sentences and tokens and removing very common words called stop words
tokenization splits a sentence into the units a model will work with
a simple tokenizer splits on spaces while a better one handles contractions such as don t abbreviations such as dr
stemming then cuts words down to a crude root so that running and runs both become run while lemmatization uses a dictionary to return the proper base form so that better becomes good
every cleaning step removes some information
lowercasing makes apple the company look like apple the fruit
removing stop words can delete the word not which turns a complaint into praise
good preprocessing is therefore a decision made for each task not a fixed recipe
how much cleaning is right for a search engine probably more than for a chatbot
language models a language model assigns a probability to a sequence of words
given the words the cat sat on the a good model should think that mat is more likely than refrigerator
this simple idea powers spelling correction speech recognition machine translation and predictive keyboards
the oldest practical language models are n gram models
a bigram model estimates the probability of a word from the one word before it and a trigram model from the two words before it
the counts come directly from a training corpus which makes the model easy to build but also means that any pair of words never seen in training receives a probability of zero
smoothing methods such as add one smoothing fix this by moving a little probability to unseen events
modern language models are neural networks
they read the text as a sequence of tokens learn a vector for each token and predict the next token using context from the whole sentence
perplexity is the standard way to compare language models the lower the perplexity the less surprised the model is by the test text
applications of nlp search engines use nlp to understand what a query means and which documents answer it
email services use it to detect spam and companies use sentiment analysis to learn whether customers are happy with a product by reading thousands of reviews in a few seconds
machine translation systems now translate between hundreds of language pairs
virtual assistants turn speech into text work out what the user wants and speak an answer back
in medicine nlp extracts diagnoses and medicines from doctors notes so that researchers can study patients at a scale that was impossible by hand
in education automatic tools check grammar grade short answers and suggest reading material
in law and finance nlp helps professionals search long contracts and news feeds
almost every one of these applications combines the same basic ingredients clean text a good representation and a model trained on enough examples
challenges and ethics human language is full of ambiguity
the sentence i saw her duck can mean two different things and the word bank can mean a river bank or a place that keeps money
sarcasm slang spelling mistakes and code switching between languages make the task harder again
languages with few digital texts including many south asian languages have far fewer tools than english
models learn from the text they are given so they also learn its biases
a system trained on biased data can repeat stereotypes about gender religion or nationality
privacy is another concern because personal messages and medical notes must be handled with great care
engineers have a responsibility to test their systems on different groups of people before they are used
accuracy numbers can hide serious mistakes and a fluent sentence is not always a true one
researchers therefore combine automatic scores with human judgement and they publish the limits of their data together with their results
conclusion natural language processing turns the written word into data that computers can use
it begins with careful preprocessing moves through tokens counts and probabilities and ends in applications that millions of people use every day
the techniques in this document from regular expressions to language models are the foundations on which larger systems are built
a student who understands why each step is needed and what it can damage is better prepared to build reliable language technology than one who only copies a recipe
